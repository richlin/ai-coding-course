# Feeding Lessons Back into the Harness — AI Coding Course

## Summary
Continuous improvement converts observed outcomes — failures, near misses, costly retries, and unusually successful workflows — into better specs, tests, rules, skills, tools, or memory. The key discipline is separating a one-time mistake from a recurring system weakness, and then targeting the improvement at the earliest point where the failure could have been prevented or detected, rather than adding another permanent rule for every isolated incident.

## Key takeaways
- Prefer one small, measurable control over many new instructions, and change one control at a time so its effect can actually be evaluated.
- Assign an owner and a review date to each change, and remove controls that create noise without improving outcomes.
- Preserve an example of the failure a control addresses, so its purpose stays legible after the incident is forgotten.
- Do not turn every isolated mistake into a permanent rule, and do not blame an individual agent before inspecting the system that produced the failure.
- Do not add controls without a baseline or success measure, and do not optimize only for failures — study efficient successes too.
