# Permissions and Human Approval — AI Coding Course

## Summary
Permissions define what data and actions an agent can access; human approval reserves consequential decisions for an accountable person. Argues approval boundaries should be set by impact, reversibility, confidence, and policy — not by a uniform level of caution — because autonomous execution can amplify a mistake quickly, while excessive approval prompts train reviewers into careless, reflexive approval.

## Key takeaways
- Grant least privilege by default and expand access for a specific task rather than standing broad access.
- Require approval for destructive, production, financial, legal, or sensitive-data actions, and show approvers the proposed action, expected effect, evidence, and rollback plan.
- Expire approvals when the action or its supporting evidence changes — a stale approval is not a valid one.
- Do not add approval gates to every minor step; excessive prompting encourages careless approval rather than careful review.
- Do not let the implementing agent approve its own accepted risk, and do not treat a notification as equivalent to approval.
