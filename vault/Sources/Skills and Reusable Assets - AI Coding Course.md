# Skills and Reusable Assets — AI Coding Course

## Summary
Reusable assets — skills, templates, scripts, review rubrics, task commands, checklists — standardize a workflow the team performs repeatedly, letting the team improve one shared workflow instead of rewriting prompts for every task. Good assets encode proven practice, built from observed repetition and failure rather than hypothetical need, without hiding the judgment calls that still require a human or a model decision.

## Key takeaways
- Build assets from observed repetition and failure, not hypothetical needs, and give each one an owner, scope, version, and test examples.
- Keep deterministic work in code/scripts and judgment-heavy work in skills or rubrics — collapsing the two hides which parts are actually reliable.
- Measure adoption and correction rate rather than counting how many skills or templates the team has produced.
- Do not create an asset for a one-time problem, and do not let a template accumulate optional sections nobody uses.
- Do not hide important judgment behind an automatic command, and do not leave a reusable asset untested after the underlying workflow changes.
